Ten years, and still my favourite plot twist.

If I tried to fit every memory into one page, we'd be here until we're eighty. So here's a little piece of our story — the bits I never want to forget.

Happy birthday, my love. ♡

It still makes me laugh that our story began on a bus to a school science symposium. You were in the front row, I was left without my usual people, and somehow I ended up sitting beside a girl from another section. I can't even remember who started the conversation — but I remember telling you your English accent was different, and you telling me about your family abroad. That little conversation became something I never could've predicted.

We properly became friends in ninth standard, and then came the days I wish I could bottle up forever: the same bench, Hindi tuition, shared tiffins, wandering around school to catch glimpses of our crushes, and me cycling you to the school bus and talking to you until the very last second. And yes, the legendary slaps. I remember both. I am choosing peace and not filing a case.

Then tenth ended and you left, and suddenly we had to learn how to be best friends from a distance. Eleventh and twelfth were hard in their own ways, but you were still my anchor in NSS. Later, we chose psychology — after approximately one million conversations about whether you should choose it at all. You went to St. Teresa's, I went to Sahardaya, and life got busy and complicated, but we kept finding our way back to each other.

I still think about that movie date I had to lie to my parents for, my first time at your house, cycling back with you and your sister, and all three of us getting absolutely drenched when the rain decided to join the outing. That memory lives rent-free in my head. So do the voice notes where I tried to explain psychology concepts to you, and all the little ways we kept showing up even when we couldn't be physically present.

And then there was the day you rode all the way to the railway station just to send me off. You had your own curfew to deal with, but you still came running to give me that huge hug. I wish I'd held on a little longer. I don't think you realise how deeply that moment touched me — or maybe you do, because that's just who you are when you love someone.

There's one memory I've carried quietly, because I know how tender it is. When you lost your grandmother — someone who saw you, loved you, and understood you so deeply — I was away in Dubai. I remember being awake with you, worrying about you, wishing with everything in me that I could be beside you in person. I have sometimes wished I could turn back time just to sit next to you and hold your hand through that day. I couldn't be there physically, and I'm sorry for that. I hope you never mistake my absence that day for a lack of love. If I could revisit one moment simply to be there with you, that would be one of them. I know no words can make that loss lighter, and I would never want this to take away from your birthday. I just wanted you to know how much I wished I could have been there.

Our lives have changed a lot since those school days. We've both had to leave places, people, and versions of ourselves behind. There have been beautiful days, difficult days, misunderstandings, disagreements, and times when we've felt a little lost. I won't pretend every chapter has been easy. But I do know this: we're both still figuring life out, and we don't have to have ourselves completely sorted to keep loving each other well.

You are clever, capable, funny, stubborn in the most Mariyam way, and so much more than the hard seasons you've had to get through. I hope you find your way back to every part of yourself you miss. And I hope you know that you don't have to be exactly the same person you were at fifteen for me to love you. I miss that girl sometimes — the one who slapped me over a crush and couldn't patiently open a pizza box — but I'm rooting for the woman you're becoming, too.

Thank you for being my anchor when I felt lost, for teaching me that life doesn't always have to be taken so seriously, for making me laugh when I was stuck in my own darkness, and for staying beside me through versions of myself that weren't always easy to understand. There were times I genuinely wondered how I would've made it through without you. You've mattered to my life more than I can properly explain.

I know I'm possessive about our friendship sometimes. I know life will keep bringing new people into both our worlds, and we'll keep changing. But I never want you to mistake the distance, the busy days, or the occasional disagreement for a lack of love. You will always have a place with me — a place where you can be loved, heard, messy, silly, quiet, confused, or completely yourself without having to perform.

I hope we get a thousand more ordinary days: more movie dates, more food, more ridiculous conversations, more hugs that last longer, more school-memory rewinds, more laughing until we can't breathe, and more chances to choose this friendship as we grow. If all we can manage in a difficult season is to hold on by a thin little thread, I'll still be grateful for that thread. I believe we'll look back one day and laugh at how dramatic some of these chapters felt.

People will come and go, and life will keep moving us around. But you are one of the people I want to keep choosing — not because we've known each other for ten years, but because after all these years, I still want you in the years ahead.

Happy birthday, my best friend. Here's to the girl I met on a bus, the girl who shared her school bench with me, the girl who ran to the railway station, and every version of you still waiting to be discovered. I love you. Always have, always will.

Your Chunnu, for the long run. ♡
